## 1. The decision

Build a **direct, price-aggressive competitor** to established Swiss event-venue directories. Do not create a generic wedding blog, a CRM vendor or a multi-year “platform ecosystem.” Create a venue-discovery portal that:

1. **Costs substantially less than the higher-priced venue listing packages** of the incumbent.
2. **Provides noticeably more usable presentation and promotion for the money**: genuine room-level listings, extensive licensed photos and videos, four Swiss-market languages, promotion-ready assets, clear direct enquiries, more applicable event categories, last-minute offers and optional cross-portal distribution where the partnership actually exists.
3. **Requires almost no work from venue owners**: profiles are prepared in advance, owner clicks an invitation, confirms ownership, approves rights and publishes. No new internal workflow or CRM is mandatory.
4. **Does actual demand generation**, not only inventory collection: destination search, precise filters, thematic pages, campaign traffic, planner relationships, matched organizer briefs and measurable qualified enquiries.
5. **Offers free entry** and a single simple paid plan at **CHF 199/year** (proposed price, before applicable VAT), with no booking commission.

**Commercial objective:** Reach **61 paying venue subscriptions at CHF 199/year = CHF 12,139/year, or CHF 1,011.58 per month annualized**, before expenses, taxes and payment fees. The goal is not to become the Swiss category leader; it is to build a low-overhead, defensible side business that can produce roughly CHF 1,000/month in recurring revenue equivalent.

**The central commercial insight:** Venue owners may already have a perfectly good spreadsheet, inbox or venue management system. **Do not ask them to change it.** Our listing and marketing benefits must stand on their own. Any SaaS/CRM/CMS tools are optional secondary bonuses, never the reason a venue has to buy.

### 2.2 What we can honestly beat

- **Price versus higher-end packages:** CHF 199/year is about **79% below CHF 949/year** and **92% below CHF 2,498/year**, comparing published sticker prices before VAT. Use exact tier names and compare like-for-like periods.
- **Preparation effort:** We can prebuild a venue draft and ask owners to confirm it instead of starting from an empty form.
- **Features per CHF:** Multiple genuine rooms, relevant event categories, DE/FR/IT/EN copy, downloadable collateral, social creative and optional promotions bundled in one price **when those capabilities are actually live**.
- **Flexibility:** Direct links, no booking commission, email-based enquiries, no required CRM, transparent cancellation and deletion.
- **Customer experience:** An accurate, mobile-first listing, natural-language city/category pages, practical filtering and fast direct enquiries.

### 2.3 What we cannot pretend to beat on day one

- Established search traffic, brand familiarity or historic lead volume.
- An existing premium newsletter audience unless we build or contract one.
- Customer bookings: a qualified enquiry is not a confirmed booking.
- Placement guarantees in search results when inventory and advertising slots are limited.
- “Double the reach” or “twice the customers” without independently verifiable data.

**Hard principle:** Aggressive on price, speed, coverage and prospecting; exact and evidence-based in public claims. An avoidable competitor complaint or rights dispute can consume an entire year's target profit.

## 3. Customer and market definitions

### 3.1 Our customer is the venue advertiser

The **paying buyer** is the person responsible for event sales, marketing, hospitality operations or the advertising budget. Titles vary: owner, hotel director, F&B manager, event manager, marketing lead, restaurant proprietor.

Their thought process: “I already pay for venue visibility, and I hate spending time maintaining listings. Show me what I get, how little work it takes and whether real enquiries arrive.”

### 3.2 Our other customer is the organizer

Without organizers, venues will churn. Organizer segments:

1. **Corporate:** office managers, executive assistants, HR, event agencies, sales teams, conference planners.
2. **Private:** couples, families, private party organizers, clubs and associations.
3. **Professional intermediaries:** wedding planners, DJs, caterers, photographers, event technicians, conference agencies.

Acquisition campaigns must serve both sides. A directory with 5,000 profiles and no organizer demand is not a business.

### 3.3 Initial segment ranking

| Priority | Venue type | Rationale | First message angle |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Hotels with meeting / banquet rooms | One customer can expose several bookable spaces, higher potential value per lead | “Jeder Saal sichtbar. Ein Preis.” |
| A | Independent wedding and event venues | Beautiful visuals, varied categories, repeat portal spending | “Deine Location ist schon vorbereitet.” |
| A | Restaurants with private dining / banquet halls | Many overlook specialized event searches; strong direct email fit | “Mehr Eventanfragen ohne neues System.” |
| B | Conference centers and seminar rooms | High-intent weekday corporate demand | “Seminarräume gezielt vermarkten.” |
| B | Castles, farms, industrial lofts, unusual spaces | Strong differentiated content, niche search demand | “Mehr passende Anlässe statt mehr Streuverlust.” |
| C | Public halls / community rooms | Lower willingness to pay, potentially useful organizer supply | Free catalogue value first |

**Initial geographic concentration:** Zürich, Aargau, Zug and Bern. The user's own observation that Romandie has lower-priced competitors is a useful hypothesis, **not a verified nationwide price study**. Launch there later with French localization and a deliberate pricing test.

### 3.4 Ideal first 100 accounts

Prefer venues that:

- Have a functioning official website and real contact email.
- Have a clear events offer, not merely a restaurant table booking service.
- Have a significant room or visual portfolio.
- Already advertise for events and plausibly understand annual portal subscriptions.
- Are independently contactable by a legally permissible channel.
- Would be comfortable approving their own photographs and offer details.

Avoid the temptation to scrape the whole incumbent directory as a shortcut to a prospect list; use independently collected public business facts, permitted sources and properly reviewed outreach methods.

## 4. The offer

### 4.1 Price and payment

**Free — CHF 0**

- Accurate basic profile that the venue can claim, correct, export or remove.
- Up to six owner-approved images and one basic room.
- Relevant baseline category inclusion and an ordinary search placement.
- One supported embedded video.
- Direct organizer enquiries routed by email; no new portal inbox required.
- Basic monthly summary email and optional sign-in for edits.
- No commission on enquiries or bookings.

**Pro — CHF 199/year**, proposed launch pricing, before VAT where applicable

- Extensive licensed photo gallery with published fair-use limits rather than arbitrary paid tier caps.
- Multiple supported external video embeds and an enhanced presentation.
- Genuine bookable room profiles without a commercial per-room fee.
- All **genuinely relevant** categories, not fabricated category stuffing.
- DE/FR/IT/EN localized listing copy, editable by the owner.
- Multiple brochures and honest event-package presentation.
- Seasonal and last-minute-offer promotion capability.
- Downloadable social media creative kit and **one starter short promo video per subscription year** using approved media.
- Enhanced statistics sent by email, no mandatory dashboard.
- Prominent visibility in defined, clearly labelled sponsorship inventory **only insofar as the placement inventory exists and the product rules specify**.
- Cross-promotion to the founder's own wedding portal or external partners **only after written content permission, technical setup and actual partner availability**.
- Optional lightweight tools later, without forcing adoption.
- No commission; cancellation online; no mystery renewal charges.

**Delivery truth:** CTO-PRD places many rich Pro extras in P1, after the first launch. CMO must maintain a live **Features Available Now** matrix; never charge on a promise that is presented as live without an explicit future delivery commitment agreed with the buyer.

### 4.2 What the price means to a venue

Don't lead with “we have infinite pictures.” Lead with:

> **Ein Inserat für deine ganze Location. Alle passenden Räume. Mehr Sprachen. Mehr Werbematerial. Direkte Anfragen. CHF 199 pro Jahr.**

Supporting sentence:

> **Dein Eintrag ist schon vorbereitet. Du prüfst ihn, gibst deine Bilder frei und entscheidest selbst. Kostenlos starten.**

### 4.3 Reasons to believe, ranked by purchasing strength

1. **Prebuilt profile**: immediate visible proof, zero blank form.
2. **Cheap enough to try**: CHF 199/year versus named higher competitor tiers, not a claim of equal traffic.
3. **A better-looking and more complete venue presence**: room cards, galleries, useful filters, brochures.
4. **More marketable places and occasions**: room-level search, relevant categories, multilingual pages.
5. **Free marketing assets**: approved short video, social images, QR, factsheet.
6. **Actual distribution and enquiries**: demonstrate honestly, track by source.
7. **Bonus software**: entirely optional; low in the sales pitch.

### 4.4 No fake scarcity or bait-and-switch

- Don't say “already published” if the page is a **private preview**.
- Don't call an unclaimed listing “verified” or imply venue endorsement.
- Don't say “free forever” if material terms can change for existing customers.
- Don't advertise unlimited server-hosted video if we offer unlimited **embeds**.
- Don't promise a partner-network feature that is not active.
- Don't force the customer to cancel another vendor or falsely imply the incumbent package has no value.

## 6. The acquisition engine: prepared listings

### 6.1 The exact supply-side funnel

`Target discovered → eligibility + legal review → draft prepared → preview QA → permitted outreach → personalized preview visit → claim starts → ownership verified → rights approved → free listing live → venue receives value → Pro purchase → renewal`

**Critical commercial principle:** Give venue owners a **finished preview**, not a promise that some importer might work after they sign up.

### 6.2 The profile preview landing page

Mandatory visible elements:

- Venue name, canton, business type, website source attribution.
- Accurate facts with explicit placeholders for unconfirmed capacities and amenities.
- Clear **“Noch nicht vom Betreiber bestätigt”** when applicable.
- Photos shown only if the rights basis covers showing them at that step; otherwise neutral placeholders and private owner-review candidates where lawful.
- Primary CTA: **“Gratis übernehmen”**.
- Secondary CTA: **“Angaben korrigieren”**.
- No credit card to claim or publish a Free listing.
- Direct explanation of Pro upgrade; no implied payment obligation.
- Visible contact, privacy and profile removal links.

### 6.3 Prebuild production standard

- First 100 target profiles checked for name, legal business identity, website and location.
- Confidence-tag capacity, amenities, seasonality and pricing; do not invent missing facts.
- Original copy derived from verifiable facts, not lightly rewritten competitor text.
- Photo rights approval before public publication; owner media authorization should cover the desired use and derivatives.
- One valid venue per physical property, with distinct rooms underneath, not manufactured duplicates.
- Profile QA score: identity correct, category correct, address correct, CTA works, no copyright gap, venue isn't permanently closed.

### 6.4 Outreach channels ranked

1. **Permissioned existing venue relationships** from the founder's own wedding portal and legitimate contacts where marketing permission applies.
2. **Targeted physical direct mail** with a personalized QR and exact preview link; avoid misleading invoice-like designs.
3. **Permissible one-to-one business outreach** under applicable Swiss rules; respect directory markings, objections and do-not-contact preferences.
4. **Organic inbound** from comparative and price pages, supplier SEO and referrals.
5. **Retargeting** only where lawful consent and tracking configuration supports it.

The claimed 5,000–6,000 existing contact records are not assumed to be consented marketing subscribers. Audit origin, purpose and suppressions first. Do not bulk-send promotional email to harvested addresses.

### 6.5 Initial 100-account outbound test

Divide the pilot into comparable 25-account cohorts:

| Cohort | Message / format | What we learn |
|---|---|---|
| A | Personalized postal letter + printed profile screenshot + QR | Whether seeing the ready-made profile drives visits |
| B | Postal letter + side-by-side price comparison + QR | Whether price drives more claims than preview alone |
| C | Allowed direct contact from a permissioned relationship, with preview | Whether trust outperforms cold traffic |
| D | Partner / referral introduction to the venue | Whether warm distribution is scalable |

**Tracking:** campaign code, account ID, contact eligibility, send date, returned mail, preview visit, claim, rights approval, publish, first lead, upgrade and retention. Compare by **paid venues per CHF spent**, not by likes or raw clicks alone.

## 7. Organizer acquisition: the second half of the business

### 7.1 Why supply does not equal demand

Generating 500 pages does not automatically create 500 useful SEO assets, and a venue will not renew at CHF 199 merely because its page exists. The growth loop must be:

`Useful search demand → real organizer → suitable venues → qualified enquiry → venue values channel → Pro conversion / renewal → richer inventory → better search quality`

### 7.2 Search-intent clusters

Prioritize search themes with an actual planning task:

- `hochzeitslocation zürich`, `hochzeitslocation aargau`, `scheune hochzeit schweiz`
- `seminarraum zürich 30 personen`, `tagungsraum zug`, `konferenzraum aargau`
- `eventlocation zürich 100 personen`, `firmenanlass location bern`
- `weihnachtsessen firma zürich`, `private dining zürich`
- `partyraum aargau`, `geburtstagslocation zug`

**Do not publish hundreds of city × category permutations with no real venue content.** Each landing page must answer a real question and have suitable, verified inventory and useful filters. No search-engine doorway pages.

### 7.3 Priority organizer pages

| Page archetype | Promise | Data it must contain | Primary CTA |
|---|---|---|---|
| City + occasion | Practical locations in a city | Genuine venues, locations, approximate size, room types | “Passende Locations ansehen” |
| Canton + size | Places fitting guest count | Verified capacities / configurations | “Nach Personenanzahl filtern” |
| Corporate event guide | Suitable options by occasion | Setup, audiovisual, catering and access **only if verified** | “Anfrage senden” |
| Last-minute dates | Actual posted open dates | Valid dates, expiry, owner confirmation status | “Verfügbarkeit anfragen” |
| Shortlist comparison | Compare three to five places | True differences, no fabricated prices | “Alle passenden anfragen” |

### 7.4 The reverse-venue-search attack

Headline for organizers: **“Eine Anfrage. Bis zu fünf passende Locations.”**

Flow:

1. Organizer selects city/region, date flexibility, guest count, event type and optional budget.
2. Show three to five genuinely eligible venues.
3. Obtain clear consent to forward the brief to the named recipients, not a blanket hidden lead sale.
4. Deliver to the existing venue email addresses.
5. Let venues reply normally; no new user interface required.
6. Ask organizer whether venues responded and whether any booking resulted, as voluntary feedback.

**Do not monetize by selling the same enquiry to dozens of suppliers.** That undermines both venue trust and organizer experience.

### 7.5 Paid search, but only with a cost cap

Start with narrow, high-intent exact/phrase search tests in selected cantons and specific events. Avoid broad paid traffic to generic tourism phrases, wedding inspiration and “free venues.” Route to optimized curated results, not the homepage.

Pilot spend proposal: **CHF 150–300** total for an initial measurement tranche, contingent on working enquiry flow, conversion tracking and a useful landing page. Stop when the campaign cannot demonstrate real relevant visits and enquiries or when cost per lead is commercially implausible.

No invented CPC, CTR or conversion rates are included: those must be observed in the Swiss auction and actual site analytics.

### 7.6 Partnerships and distribution

**Founder-operated channels:** The existing wedding-venue portal is a strong potential acquisition asset. Validate audience overlap, copyright grants and SEO canonical strategy before syndicating. The platform's job is to reuse legitimate traffic, not clone identical indexable pages on multiple domains.

**External channels to approach:** Independent wedding planners, DJs, caterers, corporate event organizers, hotel/event associations, regional tourism sites, photographers and equipment vendors. Offer a simple co-branded search page or referral link where reciprocal value exists.

**Partner offer:** “Give your clients a free venue shortlist and get a linkable resource. If you refer an organizer or venue, attribution is transparent.” Never promise partner impressions before confirmed publication.

### 7.7 Content that actually converts

Each piece answers an organizer buying decision:

- Where can we host 60 guests near Zug with indoor backup?
- Which seminar rooms have confirmed wheelchair access?
- Which venues offer multiple breakout rooms?
- How to evaluate a venue proposal and compare hidden costs?

Use verified local data and examples. Avoid recycled “Top 10 locations” fluff with dubious facts.

## 9. Funnel instrumentation and definitions

### 9.1 One canonical funnel

| Stage | Definition | Events / system source | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Targeted | Qualified independent venue prospect | `prospect_qualified` in campaign console | CMO |
| Reached | Permissible outreach delivered / meaningful channel exposure | Postal / consented / partner log | CMO |
| Previewed | Unique venue preview opened | `venue_preview_opened` | CTO |
| Claim started | Owner engages claim process | `claim_started` | CTO |
| Claimed | Authorized business ownership verified | `claim_verified` | CTO |
| Published | Public page authorized and live | `venue_published` | CTO |
| Engaged | Venue gets report or meaningful organizer interaction | `listing_engaged` etc. | CTO |
| Lead qualified | Authentic, relevant request delivered | `inquiry_delivered` plus qualification | Joint |
| Pro purchased | Successfully paid annual plan | `pro_paid` | CTO |
| Renewed | Paid for next annual term | billing provider + event | Joint |

**Critical metric:** *Paid venues with at least one genuine qualified enquiry / paying venues*. It tells us whether renewals have any real prospect.

### 9.2 Qualified enquiry definition

A qualified enquiry is a **unique, real, reachable organizer** with plausible event type, date/date range and party size, within the venue's declared criteria, delivered successfully to its chosen contact point, and not spam, a duplicate or a test. It **does not** mean the venue replied, gave an offer or booked the event. Budget is optional unless the venue requires it.

### 9.3 Dashboard KPIs

**Supply:** targeted venues, preview-ready percent, claimed percent, published percent, rights-approved gallery percent, corrections/complaints, active Pro count, upgrade rate, paid churn, retained revenue.

**Demand:** organic relevant landings, search-to-listing CTR, no-results searches, listing-to-enquiry rate, delivered qualified enquiries, median leads per claimed venue, lead distribution, enquiry complaint rate, organizer satisfaction.

**Acquisition economics:** attributable cost per reachable venue, preview, claim, published listing, Pro sale, qualified lead; campaign-level gross revenue; paid search burn; email deliverability; refund exposure.

**Do not use vanity counts** like total auto-generated records or page impressions as evidence of customer value.

| **61** | **CHF 12,139** | **CHF 1,011.58** |

**This is revenue, not profit or reliable monthly cash flow.** Annual subscriptions may be paid at irregular times, and VAT, refunds, payment processing, ads and hosting reduce take-home economics.

### 10.2 Break-even and money discipline

The business must survive as a side project at low annual prices. Initial control assumptions (budgets, not forecasts):

| Cost bucket | Proposed starting ceiling / rule |
|---|---|
| Basic hosting and delivery | Set a measured monthly ceiling; aim to keep pilot operations under CHF 100/month when practical |
| Cold postal pilot | CHF 100–300 depending on volume/production; require attribution |
| Initial organizer search ads | CHF 150–300, released only after useful search and lead capture |
| Video production | Founder-created core advertisement; one template-based venue video included in Pro; cap render runs |
| Paid professional review | Budget separately for Swiss legal/tax guidance before bulk data or advertising campaigns |
| Human onboarding | Strive for self-service; track founder minutes per claim and per account |

**Important:** A CHF 199 first-year subscription cannot support CHF 200 of bespoke support or a large CAC. Implement a cost cap per prospect, not unlimited “brand awareness.”

### 10.3 Funnel math: scenarios, not predictions

Define:

- `R` = legitimate reached venue prospects.
- `V` = percent who open their preview.
- `C` = percent of preview visitors who claim/publish.
- `P` = percent of claimed venues who buy Pro.
- `Paid = R × V × C × P`.

| Scenario (assumptions only) | Reach → preview | Preview → claim | Claim → pay | Paid customers per 1,000 reached |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Conservative | 15% | 25% | 10% | 4 (3.75 expected) |
| Working | 30% | 40% | 20% | 24 |
| Strong | 40% | 50% | 30% | 60 |

This makes the problem clear. **The user's 5,000–6,000 known contacts are not automatically 5,000–6,000 legally reachable prospects, and high conversions cannot be assumed.** We need pilot data before extrapolation. At the *working* scenario, 61 paying customers would require about 2,542 reached prospects. At the *strong* scenario, just over 1,000. Both are hypotheses, not assurances.

### 10.4 Unit economics scorecard

Track:

- Gross first-year value per Pro customer: **CHF 199 before applicable VAT**.
- Recognized contribution per subscription: CHF 199 **minus** refunds, actual fees, incremental storage/media/email costs, acquisition spend and support time valued at a realistic hourly cost.
- Acquisition payback: aim for first-year gross contribution to comfortably exceed fully loaded acquisition cost; don't assume automatic renewals.
- Operating margin sensitivity: number of founder support minutes per paying venue is often more important than storage cost.
- Long-term renewals: measure after 12 months; don't make retention claims before a cohort reaches renewal.

## 11. Launch timeline and decision gates

Do not promise fixed completion dates; this is a **relative sequence of work** with explicit go/no-go gates.

### Phase 0 — Prove buyers care before scaling

**Deliverables:**

- Clean competitor evidence file (dated prices and features) and legal review of comparisons.
- 30 independently verified venue candidates across primary cantons.
- 10 venue operator conversations, 10 genuine organizer search tasks.
- Simple visual mock-up of preview and premium venue page.
- Written tier matrix, cancellation policy, media licence and definition of qualified enquiries.

**Gate:** At least 10 operators find prepared previews valuable enough to claim/consider; the organizer workflow answers real searches. If the response is “nice listing but why pay?” test lead value before more design work.

### Phase 1 — Acquire the first 100 useful free profiles

**Deliverables:**

- 100 accurate public or private preview profiles, with proper publication rights.
- Venue claim and correction flow, direct email enquiries.
- 100-account targeted outbound test as described earlier.
- 20–30 carefully selected organizer searches in Zürich/Aargau/Zug/Bern with useful landing pages.
- First owner-visible monthly report and basic attribution.

**Gate:** At least 20 genuinely engaged venue prospects and real organizer enquiries; iterate if the marketplace gets visits but no relevant form submissions.

### Phase 2 — Sell the first 10 Pro plans

**Deliverables:**

- Checkout and published actual Pro feature inventory.
- Live package comparison page that has been source checked.
- First 10 paying accounts, with explicit feedback on why they bought.
- Fix top three reasons for not claiming/upgrading.
- At least one case study only if a venue voluntarily verifies the details.

**Gate:** **10 real payments**, credible repeatable reasons for purchase, legal and operational burden compatible with CHF 199 per year. “People liked the idea” is not a gate.

### Phase 3 — Extend to 500 quality venues and richer Pro

**Deliverables:**

- Roll out actual multi-room, multi-language, short video, PDFs, last-minute capability.
- Expand quality catalogue towards 500 only with review and useful search demand.
- Build 1–3 real referral/distribution agreements.
- Run measured search tests and close the content gaps found in search logs.

**Gate:** Qualified enquiry quality remains high, first renewal signals are not negative, and customer acquisition is reasonably efficient.

### Phase 4 — Reach 61 active paying venues

**Deliverables:**

- Repeat highest-return supply outreach channel.
- Prioritize under-served micro-regions and seasonal categories based on actual queries.
- Expand into French/Italian after translation QA and market-specific willingness-to-pay validation.
- Raise or segment pricing only on real evidence; do not introduce five confusing tiers.

**Gate:** **61 active paid subscribers**, stable delivery, no significant rights or complaints backlog, reported monthly annualized gross above CHF 1,000.

## 12. Marketing experiments and stop criteria

| Experiment | Hypothesis | Primary metric | Failure / stop trigger |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prepared preview vs blank signup | Finished preview increases claims | Claims / eligible reached | Little/no lift after matched cohort |
| Price headline vs no comparison | Price framing increases qualified claims | Pro purchases / eligible reached | Higher traffic but no paid conversion, or legal/comparison concerns |
| Room-first page vs gallery-first | Room cards increase enquiry intent for multi-room venues | Enquiry rate per relevant landing | No measurable improvement / duplicated venue confusion |
| “Free first” vs “Pro first” | Free claim reduces initial friction | Published claims and later paid rate | Many unmaintained pages, negligible paid interest |
| One-year refund promise | Guarantee could reduce price anxiety | Incremental paid conversion net of refunds | Fraud, unsupported lead promises or negative contribution margin |
| Reverse match | A single brief generates more useful opportunities | Qualified delivered enquiries per organizer brief | Spam reports, poor match fit or venue overload |
| Video asset as bonus | Included promo video improves conversion | Upgrades among eligible venues | Creation/support cost exceeds benefit |
| German canton microsites | Geographic specificity increases organizer quality | Qualified enquiry / organic landing | Low-quality thin pages or poor indexation |
| Referral from wedding portal | Existing audience converts more cheaply | Qualified organizer leads and Pro sales | Cannibalized SEO/duplicate content |

**No automatic winner from a tiny A/B test.** Make decisions from direction, cohort quality, observed costs and obvious product/user feedback, not bogus statistical certainty.

## 14. Retention without creating a CRM dependency

A venue need not log in more than once. Retention mechanisms must be **asynchronous** and naturally useful:

- Monthly email summary: impressions where measurable, meaningful gallery interactions, delivered enquiries and one practical correction suggestion.
- Owner-approved “new room” or “seasonal offer” update from a simple email link, with secure authorization.
- One-click edit/correct listing and media approval from mobile.
- Annual renewal reminder with transparent price and cancellation link.
- Quarterly optional “your listing vs missing information” completeness message (do not email too often).
- Real analytics, not inflated “estimated reach.” Distinguish unique clicks from qualified contacts.
- Owner can export content and close account; good faith is part of the pitch.

**Annual customer health segments:**

| Segment | Trigger | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Healthy | Qualified enquiries and accurate profile | Share report; don't oversell |
| Needs attention | Views but almost no enquiries | Suggest specific photos/facts or improve category fit |
| No exposure | Few useful views | Improve search distribution or tell the truth; don't bury it |
| At risk | No enquiries, early cancellation question | Review actual results, offer free downgrade, don't trap |
| Champion | Verified positive outcomes | Ask permission for a case study/referral |

## 15. PR, reputation and competitor-response planning

### 15.1 Launch story

**“Swiss venue owners can now activate a prepared profile for free and get comprehensive venue presentation for CHF 199/year.”**

Pitch to relevant Swiss SME, hospitality and regional business publications with an actual product demo. Do not pitch “we will destroy a monopoly.” That is internal competitive energy, not a public claim.

## 16. Legal and ethical campaign requirements

**Swiss legal counsel should review the actual launch flows and copy. This document is a commercial spec, not legal advice.**

1. **Photography and text:** A venue's permission alone may be insufficient if the photographer holds rights or restricted use. Obtain rights adequate for listing, resized copies, social collateral, video derivatives, translation/cropping and partner distribution. Do not clone a competitor's database. See [Swiss IPI photo guidance](https://www.ige.ch/de/etwas-schuetzen/urheberrecht/ein-werk-nutzen/fotografienschutz) and [Eventlokale site terms](https://www.eventlokale.ch/site/extern/impressum.html).
2. **Comparative advertising:** State exact competitor product, evidence, date and any material differences; avoid misleading comparisons, unsupported performance claims, brand confusion and gratuitous disparagement. See [SECO unfair competition basics](https://www.seco.admin.ch/de/grundlagen-uwg).
3. **Unsolicited messages:** A public business email is not automatically marketing consent. Review Swiss email, telephone and direct-marketing rules, existing-customer exception, opt-outs and contact-source provenance. See [SECO competition topics](https://www.seco.admin.ch/de/uwg-themen) and relevant anti-spam guidance.
4. **Directory solicitations:** Clearly label invitations as offers, not invoices, register obligations or renewal notices. SECO flags misleading directory solicitations. See [SECO topics](https://www.seco.admin.ch/de/uwg-themen).
5. **Privacy:** Keep only necessary contact data, honor complaints and correction/deletion requests, disclose analytics and overseas processors. See [Swiss FDPIC](https://www.edoeb.admin.ch/de/datenschutzerklaerungen-im-internet).
6. **Pricing and renewal:** State total charge, VAT treatment, billing period, cancellation policy and payment obligation in the checkout and communications. See [SECO price display FAQ](https://www.seco.admin.ch/de/faq-pbv).
7. **Review integrity:** No fake reviews, fictitious booked events, synthetic social proof or inflated lead reports.
8. **Search integrity:** No thin mass-generated search pages or doorway SEO farms; follow [Google spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies).

## 17. Operating cadence for a solo founder

### Weekly 60-minute scorecard

1. **Supply:** new preview-ready, reached, claimed, published, paid.
2. **Demand:** organizer users, valuable searches, delivered qualified enquiries, complaints.
3. **Revenue:** paid active, receipts, annualized run rate, refunds, recurring costs.
4. **Top three blockers:** photos/rights, claim friction, lead quality, or SEO mismatches.
5. **One experiment launched, one stopped, one improved.**

### Monthly decision review

- Which acquisition channel produced actual paying venues most cheaply?
- Which pages/queries produced the best organizer enquiries?
- Which venue categories got leads but could not supply available matches?
- Which Pro feature do paying operators actually name as the reason to buy?
- Are rights complaints, support minutes or promotional asset costs endangering the model?
- What do paying venues say the platform **has done**, not merely what it “could do”?

### Non-negotiable reporting rules

- Do not report a demo request as a real lead.
- Do not count enquiry delivery to five venues as five distinct organizers.
- Do not say “500 live venues” if many are unclaimed private drafts.
- Do not claim current product benefits that are only on the P1 backlog.
- Attribute annualized recurring revenue honestly, separated from cash collected and profit.

## 20. Final go / no-go criteria

**Proceed aggressively** if:

- We can show accurate, attractive, rights-cleared previews to owners.
- Enough venues claim or pay without high-touch onboarding.
- Real organizer demand produces verified qualified enquiries.
- First-year contribution per paid venue remains positive after realistic acquisition/support costs.
- Comparative messaging is defensible from dated public evidence.

**Stop or reposition** if:

- Claim rates remain weak despite good previews and direct outreach.
- Venues clearly will not pay without meaningful lead volume, and organizer acquisition is prohibitively expensive.
- Copy/rights or other legal work makes the economics unattractive.
- Most listings are generic low-value pages, and the SEO traffic never develops.
- The founder must personally serve every CHF 199 customer for hours.

**Final operating instruction:** Be a ruthless competitor on friction, price transparency and demonstrable value—not on unverified promises. The first decisive milestone is **10 real paying venues** and **real qualified organizer enquiries**. The business target is **61 annual subscriptions**. Everything else is optional.
